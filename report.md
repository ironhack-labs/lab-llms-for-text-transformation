## Finding and Reflections

In this exercise, I explored how Large Language Models can perform text transformation tasks by applying three distinct prompts to the same product review. The goal was to understand how prompt structure and intent influence the output’s tone, clarity, and format.

### Version 1 - Simplified Style
The model corrected grammar issues and simplified phrasing while preserving the review’s friendly and conversational tone.  
Result: Clear, natural, and authentic.  
Limitation: Minimal stylistic change, mostly surface-level edits.

### Version 2 — Academic Style
The review was reframed into a structured academic analysis. It emphasized product evaluation, logical flow, and formal tone.  
Result: Very professional, coherent, and analytical.  
Limitation: The tone became overly formal, losing some emotional warmth. there is also a slight risk of over-interpretation.

### Version 3 — Marketing Rewrite
The model converted the review into persuasive ad copy suitable for a product website.  
Result: Engaging, polished, and emotionally appealing.  
Limitation: there was a minor exaggeration (“indulge in the adorable charm”). I believe its a subtle form of hallucination, since the prompt shows creativity.

### Insights and Learnings
- The more specific the instruction, the more consistent and predictable the output.  
- Prompts strongly determine tone and style.
- LLMs adapt contextually, but vague prompts may lead to unexpected or overly creative results.  
- “Hallucination” risk increases when creativity or persuasion is requested.  

### Conclusion
Prompt engineering is both an art and a science. Precision guides accuracy, while tone gives shape personality.
